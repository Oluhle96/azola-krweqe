*Home Affairs: The Everyday is the Archive* begins with a simple question: what do we keep, and why?

The exhibition brings together artists and makers whose work treats the ordinary record — a photograph, a document, a garment, a room — as a form of evidence. Rather than illustrating a single history, the works allow several to sit alongside one another, unresolved.

Across the gallery, private material meets public language: the forms we fill in, the papers we inherit, the images that outlast us. The exhibition is less interested in the archive as a finished thing than in the act of keeping as a living practice.
